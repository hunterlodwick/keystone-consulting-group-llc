## 1. REVIEWS, GOOGLE BUSINESS PROFILE AND LOCAL SEARCH

**Source: BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026** (published 11 Feb 2026)
Panel: 1,002 US adult consumers via SurveyMonkey. URL:
https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey

- **97%** of consumers read reviews for local businesses.
- **41%** "always" read reviews when browsing for a business, up from **29%** the year before.
- Google's share as the review platform consumers use slipped from **83% (2025) to 71% (2026)**.
  Apple Maps nearly doubled, **14% to 27%**.
- **47%** of consumers will not use a business with fewer than **20 reviews**. Only **9%** would
  use a business with 5 reviews or fewer.
- **92%** care about star ratings. **68%** will only use a business with **4+ stars** (up from 55%).
  **31%** require **4.5+ stars** (up from 17% in a single year).
- **54%** visit the business's website after reading positive reviews, up from **32%** in 2019.
- **93%** of consumers have made a purchase after reading reviews. **27%** spent more than $1,000.
- **89%** expect a response to their review. **81%** expect a reply within a week. **32%** want a
  response by the next day (up from 18%). **19%** expect same-day (up from 6%).
- **42%** are unlikely to use a business that never replies to reviews.
- **50%** are put off by generic or templated replies.
- **82%** read AI review summaries. **42%** trust AI recommendations as much as traditional reviews.

Supporting BrightLocal SMB research (same publisher):
- Only **35%** of small businesses have a Google Business Profile.
- Only **40%** of local businesses have a dedicated website.

The "complete profile gets 7x more clicks" line is Google's own quoted figure via BrightLocal's
GMB Insights study. Treat as secondary: cite as BrightLocal quoting Google, or omit.

## 3. E-COMMERCE AND CHECKOUT

**Source: Baymard Institute**, cart abandonment research — an average of **50 separate studies**.
URL: https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate

- **70.22%** average documented online shopping cart abandonment rate.

Baymard's headline is that roughly **seven in ten** people who add to cart never finish. That is the

## 4. CRM — USE THE HONEST VERSION

**Source: Nucleus Research** (independent ROI research firm).
URL: https://nucleusresearch.com/research/single/crm-benefit-areas-with-the-greatest-roi-impact/

- Nucleus found the return on CRM investment has **declined by 37% over the last 10 years**.
- Current average return: **$3.10 per dollar spent** (still positive, but far below the old figure).

**Do NOT use "$8.71 per dollar".** That figure is from 2014, is quoted everywhere, and Nucleus has
since published the decline above. The honest and far more useful framing: CRM returns have fallen
precisely because most businesses buy software and change nothing about how they work. A CRM shaped
around an actual workflow is the fix. This is a much better story than a stale multiplier.

The circulating "29% sales increase / 34% sales productivity" figures trace to vendor material
(Salesforce) and are widely repeated secondhand. **Do not use them.**

## 5. AI ADOPTION AND THE REAL BARRIER TO IT

**Source: Federal Reserve Small Business Credit Survey, 2026 Report on Employer Firms**
(2025 survey data; 99.7% of US employer establishments covered). URL:
https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms

- **46%** of firms already use AI in their business or among employees.
- A further **15%** planned to start within the next 12 months.
- For firms planning to adopt, the top barriers were **finding tools that meet business needs (54%)**
  and **the time required to implement or train employees (37%)**.
- Of the **33%** with no plans to adopt AI, over half said it simply is not applicable to them, and
  **30%** said they prefer not to use it.

This is a Federal Reserve source saying the barrier is not willingness but tool selection and
implementation time. That is the single best justification for consulting and AI implementation work
on the entire site. Use it on both the consulting and automations-adjacent pages.

## 7. BUSINESS FINANCING (primary and strong)

**Source: Federal Reserve Small Business Credit Survey, 2026 Report on Employer Firms** (same as above)

- **60%** of firms applied for financing in the 12 months before the survey.
- The most common reasons: **operating expenses (56%)** and **pursuing an expansion or new
  opportunity (46%)**.
- Of those who applied: **42% received the full amount**, **36% received some or most**, and
  **22% received none at all**.
- Applicants at small banks were the most likely to be fully approved (**57%**).
- Applications at online fintech lenders rose from **17% (2020) to 29% (2025)**.
- **31%** of firms carry no outstanding debt. Of those with debt, **59%** used a personal guarantee.

The **22% who received none, and 36% who got only part** is the honest, powerful hook for business
loans and for consumer financing: a large share of demand goes unmet by traditional lenders. That is
who the product is for, straight from the Federal Reserve.

## 8. CONSUMER FINANCING — USE ONLY THE PRIMARY FRAMING

The internet is full of vendor blogs claiming financing lifts close rates by 18%, 20-30%, 47%, or
raises average order value 15-40%. Those are marketing pages citing unnamed "industry research".
**A specific attempt to verify the widely-repeated GreenSky "20-30% more jobs over $5,000" claim
returned no source. Do not use it and do not use any similar figure.**

Safe and primary-sourced angles for this page:
- The Fed data above: **22% of financing applicants received none** and 36% received only part. Those
  customers exist at the point of sale every day.
- **93%** of consumers have made a purchase after reading reviews, and **27%** spent over $1,000
  (BrightLocal 2026) — evidence that large-ticket purchases are normal and price is the friction.
- Frame the value structurally rather than with invented percentages: a customer who cannot pay the
  full amount today is a lost sale unless a payment plan exists. That is arithmetic, not a statistic,
  and it can be stated as such.

## 9. BUSINESS EXITING / PREP TO SELL

Exit Planning Institute: roughly **73%** of business owners plan to exit within the next 10 years,
representing about **$14 trillion** in private business value. This is widely quoted. **Verify it with
a fetch before using.** If it cannot be verified, either omit or state the general point (most owners
will sell to a buyer who values systems over personality) without a number.

The defensible structural point needs no statistic: a buyer prices what they can verify and what
keeps working without the owner. Owner dependence is the discount.
